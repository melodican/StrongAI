# The War Dossier — Retention Re-Hook Guide
*How to flatten the retention curve on every script. Built from the Pilots video
retention graph (June 2026): strong intro hold (~80% at 0:30), no cliffs, but a
steady ~10%/min bleed to ~15%. The fix is scripting, not editing.*

---

## The problem this solves

Narrated documentary scripts produce **even, measured decline** because the tone
is even and measured. There's no broken moment to fix — people leak out steadily
because nothing gives them a *fresh* reason to stay. We flatten the slope by
planting **re-hooks**: small open loops that promise a payoff a little further on,
so the viewer's reason-to-stay is constantly being renewed.

Target: lift Pilots-style videos from ~40% → ~48%+ average percentage viewed.
Retention feeds the recommendation engine, so this compounds across the catalogue.

---

## The rule: one re-hook every 60–90 seconds

At ~150 wpm narration, that's **roughly every 150–220 words** — i.e. about every
1–2 paragraphs. Don't overdo it; a re-hook every sentence reads as clickbait and
kills trust. The cadence is: *deliver value → open a small loop → deliver value →
close that loop while opening the next.*

### The intro is already working — protect it
The cold-opens hold ~80% at 30 seconds. Do **not** rewrite the openings. Keep the
"This is the dossier on..." structure. The re-hooks are for the *body*, from about
the 60-second mark onward.

---

## The five re-hook types (rotate them — don't repeat one type back-to-back)

**1. The forward-tease ("but that's not the worst part")**
Promise that something bigger is coming.
> *"...and that alone would have been enough to break most men. But it was the next
> discovery — the one beneath their feet — that truly turned the jungle into a nightmare."*

**2. The withheld answer ("the real reason was worse")**
State the obvious answer, then signal the true one is still ahead.
> *"That was the official explanation. The real reason the pilots turned away was
> something the records wouldn't admit for years."*

**3. The open question**
Pose a question the viewer now needs answered.
> *"So why did a few hundred men, with no hope of rescue, keep going back? The answer
> is the most unsettling part of the whole file."*

**4. The mid-video reset ("here's where it changes")**
A verbal "chapter break" that signals a shift and re-engages drifting attention.
> *"Up to this point, the story is one of fear. What happens next turns it on its head."*

**5. The stakes-raise ("and it was about to get worse")**
Escalate — tell them the situation is about to intensify.
> *"They thought they understood the danger. They were wrong. The ground itself was
> about to prove it."*

## Placement map (for a ~7-min / ~1,100–1,400-word body)

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | **Cold-open hook** (existing — don't touch). End on the "This is the dossier on..." line. |
| ~1:00 | **Re-hook #1** — forward-tease the single most dramatic beat of the video. |
| ~2:15 | **Re-hook #2** — withheld answer or open question tied to the next section. |
| ~3:30 | **Re-hook #3** — mid-video reset ("here's where it changes"). The halfway re-engage. |
| ~4:45 | **Re-hook #4** — stakes-raise into the climax/most-feared element. |
| ~6:00 | **Re-hook #5** — final loop: tease the "deeper truth" before the closing payoff. |
| close | Land every open loop, then the "Subscribe to The War Dossier..." CTA. |

**Golden rule: every loop you open must close.** A teased payoff that never lands
trains viewers to distrust the next tease — and tanks retention on the *following*
video. Open five loops, close five loops.

---

## Quick QA checklist before sending a script to VidRush
- [ ] Cold-open untouched, ends on "This is the dossier on..."
- [ ] 5 re-hooks, ~60–90s apart, no two adjacent of the same type
- [ ] Every opened loop is explicitly closed before the outro
- [ ] No re-hook over-promises beyond what the content delivers
- [ ] Subscribe CTA present at the close
